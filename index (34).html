Enmei · when & why people leave, and the term-renewal cycle
to
Post-term continuation: did they stay after completing 6 months?
Scoped to memberships whose term ended within the selected timeframe above. Only counts memberships that actually reached their 6-month term end without leaving early -- early leavers are excluded entirely, since this is specifically about what happens once someone completes their commitment. A plan switch/upgrade counts as "continued," not a dropout.
Dropoff timing (all genuine departures)
"Genuine" excludes upgrades, downgrades, and studio switches -- those aren't real departures. Among genuine departures in the selected range, how many months into their membership were they when they cancelled -- including early leavers who never reached term end. Dynamic range, no cap.
Dropoff after renewal
Only counts people who completed their first 6-month term and renewed, then later cancelled for real. "1 month" means they cancelled within their first month of the renewed term -- these are months into the new commitment, not the original one. A cluster near the start means the renewal itself isn't sticking; a cluster near month 6 of the new term means the same 6-month decision point is repeating.
Membership tenure mix
How long members were with us as of the selected date. Note: memberships are 6-month commitments with an option to renew, so drop-off around the 3-6/6-12 month buckets may reflect natural non-renewal, not dissatisfaction

Definitions & Methodology

How every number on the Membership Health pages is calculated
Ongoing members
Started and not yet cancelled. Includes frozen and payment-failure memberships -- broader than "active."
Active (status mix chart / table)
Strict, real-time status from Mariana Tek. Excludes frozen, payment failure, terminated, etc.
MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)
Exact sum of each membership's real renewal_rate, right now, for memberships whose live status is strictly "active" -- not an estimate or average. Frozen, payment-failure, terminated, and all other non-active statuses are excluded, since those memberships are not being charged their normal rate while in that state. Assumes all billing is monthly (confirmed with the business). Does not include the £30 freeze fee.
Cancelled
Count of memberships whose real cancellation date falls within the timeframe selected on each page.
New / Cancelled / Frozen over time
Per week: new = started that week; cancelled = cancellation date fell that week; frozen = under an active freeze at that point in time. All from real dates.
Growth % / Avg weekly churn rate
Growth = (Ongoing at end - Ongoing at start) / Ongoing at start. Churn rate = the average of each individual week's own churn rate (that week's cancellations / that week's active count) across the selected range -- NOT total cancellations divided by a single starting count. That simpler approach breaks down for long lookbacks: the starting denominator can be tiny (e.g. early in the business) while cancellations accumulate from many people who joined later, producing nonsense numbers over 100%. Averaging weekly rates keeps this meaningful regardless of how long a range you pick.
Compare to (Previous Period / Previous Month / Same Period Last Year)
The comparison window always matches the length of your selected range, not a whole calendar unit -- e.g. MTD on 18 July (1-18 July) compares to 1-18 June for "Previous Month," not all of June. On Overview and Churn & Retention metric cards, only applies as small delta badges -- not overlaid on the weekly trend charts (New/Cancelled/Frozen, Cancellation Reasons), since those are tied to real calendar dates and a true overlay would need re-indexing to relative week numbers to be readable. The Dropoff timing chart is the exception -- its buckets are already categorical (Month 1, 2, 3...), so a real overlay works well there.
Post-term continuation: did they stay after completing 6 months?
Cohort = memberships whose 6-month term end date falls within the selected timeframe AND has already passed. Critically, anyone who cancelled BEFORE reaching their term end is excluded entirely from this cohort -- they never completed the term, so they're not part of this question. Within the remaining cohort: "Continued" = never cancelled, or cancelled but it was a plan switch/upgrade (not a real departure). "Did not continue" = a genuine cancellation that happened at or after their term end. Because this is scoped by term end date (not cancellation date), the numbers genuinely change as you adjust the timeframe above.
Dropoff after renewal
For the "did not continue" group only (from Post-term continuation above): months between their term end date (when they renewed) and their actual cancellation date. "Renewed +1mo" means 1 month into the NEW term, not the original 6-month commitment -- these people already completed their first term successfully. Dynamic range -- extends as far as real data goes, no artificial cap.
Dropoff timing (all genuine departures)
Months between membership start date and cancellation date, for every genuine departure in the selected range -- including early leavers who never reached their 6-month term end, unlike the chart above. Excludes upgrades, downgrades, and studio switches, same as everywhere else on this page. Shown as % share of departures per month, so the shape is comparable even when comparing periods with very different total volumes. Dynamic month range, no cap.
Caveat: tier classification
Tiers are assigned by matching keywords in each membership's name. A few edge cases were manually resolved with the business.
Caveat: excluded from all views
"1 Week Unlimited" (a real intro offer) and "Recovery/Compression Boots" (a client perk) are excluded everywhere. Mariana Tek's own "is_intro_offer" flag does NOT reliably mark 1 Week Unlimited -- exclusion is done by name instead.
Caveat: upgrades, downgrades & studio switches are not counted as churn
When a cancellation is followed within 3 days by a new membership for the same customer, it's treated as a plan change or studio switch, not a real departure. These pairs are excluded from both cancelled and new-signup counts everywhere. If a cancellation is labeled upgrade/downgrade/new_home_studio but no matching new membership was found within 3 days, it still counts as real churn -- it shows up in the "Other" bucket of the cancellation-reasons chart, since "Other" catches everything not explicitly cost/moving/injury. This keeps the reasons breakdown always summing to the same total as the top-level "Cancelled" count.
MRR over time (Revenue page)
Each calendar month shows the last available weekly snapshot within that month (not a sum of all weeks -- revenue is a point-in-time total, summing weeks would multiply-count the same ongoing revenue). Excludes frozen memberships to match the live MRR definition exactly.
Caveat: historical MRR doesn't exclude payment failures
Unlike frozen periods (which have clean start/end dates we can reconstruct), there's no clear "payment failure resolved" date in the data -- so MRR over time can't precisely exclude payment-failure weeks the way live "right now" MRR does. This means historical MRR may run very slightly higher than true MRR during weeks with active payment failures. Frozen exclusion is exact; payment-failure exclusion is not yet possible historically.
Caveat: freeze fees not tracked
The £30 freeze fee (first month free) is not yet reflected in MRR or revenue figures.
Caveat: "Nearing term end" and "Payment risk" lists are ID-only
No names shown, since the underlying Google Sheet is published to web. Staff look up customer_id directly in Mariana Tek for outreach. A private, non-published sheet with real names is a planned follow-up.
Revenue concentration flag
Fires when the single largest membership tier accounts for 50% or more of total MRR -- a real exposure if that tier's demand or pricing changes.

Intro Offers: Definitions & Methodology

How every number on the Intro Offers page is calculated
What "intro offer started" means -- purchase date, not first check-in
The timeframe/date filter is based on when someone PURCHASED their intro offer (1 Week Unlimited membership, or Welcome 3 credits), not when they first attended a class. Someone could buy on a Monday and not actually check in until Wednesday -- the "start" date used everywhere on this page is the Monday purchase, not the Wednesday check-in.
What "timeframe" filters on
Filters by when the intro offer STARTED (purchase date), not when someone converted. So "28D" means "people who purchased an intro offer in the last 28 days" -- their eventual outcome is tracked however long it actually took, even beyond the 28-day window. This is exactly why very recent starters need the "Pending" category below, rather than being counted as a definitive "No Further Purchase."
"Hopped" (Tried Another Intro Offer)
Someone who, instead of converting, went and tried the OTHER intro offer product -- e.g. did Welcome 3, then later tried 1 Week Unlimited -- without ever buying a real membership or a 5+ credit/class pack.
"Pending (recent, no outcome yet)"
Anyone whose intro offer started within the last 30 days AND who hasn't converted, hopped, or made a casual repeat purchase yet is shown as "Pending," not "No Further Purchase." They simply haven't had enough time to show their true outcome -- lumping them in with genuine non-converters would understate the real conversion rate for recent cohorts. After 30 days with no further activity, they're counted as a real "No Further Purchase."
"Converted" (lifetime outcome)
Not just their very next purchase -- tracks forward however long it takes, using real purchase dates. Someone who took 4 months to buy a membership still counts as converted. We do NOT wait for someone's intro offer "period" to technically finish (e.g. the 1-week window ending) before checking their outcome -- any real subsequent purchase counts immediately, whenever it happens.
Outcome classification (priority order, mutually exclusive)
1) Converted to Membership -- ever bought a real recurring membership. 2) Converted to Pack -- ever bought a 5+ credit/class pack (checked only if not a membership conversion). 3) Tried Another Intro Offer -- bought the OTHER intro offer without ever converting. 4) Casual Re-engagement Only -- bought something again (drop-ins, small packs) but never converted or hopped. 5) Pending or No Further Purchase -- see above.
Converted within 7/30/90 days
Cumulative % of CONVERTERS (not all starters) who converted within that many days of their own intro offer starting -- not a calendar date. Chosen over a single raw "median days" figure since a normalized rate is easier to compare across different time periods and between the two intro offer types. The Time to Convert chart itself is also shown as a % of converters per bucket, for the same reason.
Studio filtering
Only meaningfully applies to "1 Week Unlimited" (a membership, with real purchase-location data). "Welcome 3" is a credit product usable at any studio, so it's always "All Studios" by design -- not a data gap. Picking a specific studio will naturally show 0 Welcome 3 starters, which is expected.
Head-to-head & studio comparison tables
These deliberately ignore certain page-level toggles so they can always show a real side-by-side comparison: the head-to-head table ignores the intro-type dropdown (it needs both to compare), and the studio table ignores the studio toggle (it always shows Chelsea vs. Marylebone together). Both still respect the timeframe.
Caveat: Welcome 3 attendance data is ~95% complete
Real check-ins are matched via credit_id=2356, which covers the large majority of real Welcome 3 purchases. A small number of edge-case purchases under different internal credit IDs, and any complimentary/comped intro credits, are not included in attendance figures.
Caveat: "days between visits" is all-time only
Unlike everything else on this page, average/median days between visits cannot currently be filtered by timeframe/studio -- it's calculated from visit-to-visit gaps, which live at a different level of detail than the per-starter data everything else here recomputes from live.
Expiring & expired widget -- a deliberately separate system
This widget uses Mariana Tek's own pre-built "Expiring Intro Offer" reports and its "Conversion Purchase" field -- NOT our own lifetime-outcome classification used everywhere else on this page. "Expiring soon" = ≤3 days left (both offer types). "Low usage" (Welcome 3 only) = 1 or fewer classes remaining, regardless of time left -- a fast-finisher who burns through all 3 classes in 2 days is a hotter lead than a simple time-based nudge. "Recently expired" is capped at the last 30 days -- a safety net for the gap between weekly outreach runs, not an all-time backlog. Anyone who never activated their offer (no classes taken yet) is excluded entirely -- that's a separate workflow. This widget shows aggregate counts only; the real client-level outreach list (with names) is generated by a separate, existing weekly automation the team already uses.
Summer Strong -- standalone, separate from everything above
A temporary 2-week-unlimited, £150 promo, deliberately kept OUT of the main 1 Week Unlimited/Welcome 3 system rather than integrated as a permanent third intro type. Reuses the same purchase-timeline data (no extra pull needed), but with its own outcome categories -- including "Repeated Summer Strong," since buying the same promo again isn't a real conversion. Expiration is assumed to be 14 days after the start date -- this has NOT been validated against Mariana Tek's own commitment-length field, since it's a brand-new, fixed-length product rather than the usual 6-month renewing commitment. If the expiring/outreach numbers look wrong once real data comes in, this assumption is the first place to check. No timeframe/studio filters -- always shows full history, aggregate counts only, no names.

Also excluded from Membership Health entirely (same treatment as 1 Week Unlimited, for the same reason): counting a guaranteed 2-week product as a normal membership would create a fake "cancellation" for every single signup exactly 2 weeks later, inflating churn rate and polluting cancellation-reason stats with an event that isn't real dissatisfaction. "Revenue collected" above is an estimate (starters × £150 flat price), not a real per-transaction pull -- it won't reflect any discounts or refunds on individual purchases.

Class Performance Definitions & Methodology

Enmei · how these numbers are built
Data sources
Class-level utilization comes from Mariana Tek's "Class Session Utilization Details" report (report 294) — one row per class that actually took place. Cancelled classes come from a separate pull of the class_sessions endpoint, filtered to a non-null cancellation timestamp. These two sources are deliberately NOT joined or reconciled — confirmed during build that cancelled classes never appear in report 294 at all, so "classes held" and "classes cancelled" are two independent counts, not complementary halves of one number.
Excluded from all figures
"Full Body" (a training session, not a coach-led class — confirmed to always have no instructor logged) and "Recovery — Compression Boots" (a perk, not a real class — identified by name rather than a reliable data flag, since roughly a quarter of these rows do have an instructor logged, so a null-instructor rule alone wouldn't catch it cleanly). Both are excluded from class counts, utilization averages, and the coach scorecard.
Admin Holds
Counted as a paid visit within "% Paid Visits" — these are mainly private classes where staff manually hold spots. This is validated but genuinely rare (a handful of real occurrences found across two months of data), so it won't move headline numbers much, but is accurate on the classes where it applies. "Unavailable Holds" (e.g. broken equipment) are deliberately NOT counted — that's lost capacity, not a paid visit.
Data quality flag: unassigned instructor
A class with no instructor logged that ISN'T Full Body or Recovery is flagged as a genuine data gap (a missed instructor assignment), not silently excluded. These classes are dropped from the coach scorecard (there's no one to attribute them to) but still count toward total classes held.
Utilization heatmap
Groups classes by class start time (rows) × day of week (columns), averaging the selected metric (% Utilization, % Paid Visits, or raw class count) across the selected studio and timeframe. Color scale is fixed 0–100%, recalibrated so a strong class (80%+) reads as visually "hot" rather than saving full intensity only for values near 100% — comparable across studios and across the multi-studio comparison view, which supports any number of studios (not capped at two).
Performance flags
Flags a day/time slot as underperforming or exceptional when it's at least 12 points away from the "Avg utilization" figure shown at the top of the page — the same overall average, for the same selected studio & timeframe. Points here means percentage points: 65% vs. 40% is a 25-point gap, not a "25% change." Only flags once a slot has had at least 3 classes in the period, so one unusually quiet or busy day doesn't get treated as a pattern. The trend compares the first half of the selected period to the second half (needs at least 2 classes in each half to say anything) — this respects whatever timeframe toggle is selected, unlike Momentum below, which is intentionally fixed.
Coach scorecard & utilization lift
"Lift" compares a coach's actual utilization on a given class to the average utilization for that exact studio + time slot, over the same selected timeframe — so a coach's 7am class is judged against other 7am classes, not against a busier evening slot. A class co-taught by more than one instructor counts fully toward each instructor's numbers, not split between them.
Momentum (rising/falling)
Unlike the rest of this section, the Momentum leaderboard uses a fixed 14-day vs. 56-day utilization lift comparison, recomputed daily by the data pipeline — it does NOT follow the timeframe toggle on the Coach Performance page. This is intentional: momentum is meant to answer "who's trending up or down recently," which needs a fixed short-vs-long window, not an arbitrary date range.
Known gaps
Shoreditch is not yet open, so it will show no data here. Cancelled classes cannot currently be attributed to a specific instructor — the class_sessions data for a cancelled class does not reliably include who was scheduled to teach it — so cancellations appear only as studio-level totals, not on the coach scorecard. Performance flags need at least 3 classes in a slot before flagging it, so a brand-new class or timeframe with sparse data may show fewer flags than you'd expect — that's the sample-size guard working as intended, not a bug.
